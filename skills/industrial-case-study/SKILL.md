# Industrial AI Case Study Skill

## Goal
Turn a real deployment, pilot, or approved demonstration into a credible technical-business case study. Never present a conceptual demo as a customer deployment or an expected result as measured.

## Required inputs
Customer/site and naming permission; industry, process, and asset; problem and existing workflow; approved solution description; architecture and implementation scope; dates; baseline, evaluation period, measurement method, results; approved quotations; limitations; publication permissions.

If information is missing, insert a placeholder and ask for evidence rather than filling gaps.

## Structure
1. Outcome-oriented title, accurate to evidence
2. Customer and operating context
3. Challenge and consequence
4. Previous process and baseline
5. Requirements and constraints
6. Solution and architecture
7. Implementation and integration
8. Human role, controls, security, and safety
9. Results with method and timeframe
10. Limitations and lessons
11. Relevant next step / CTA
12. Methodology, definitions, and permissions as needed

## Metrics discipline
For each metric record definition, unit, baseline, post-intervention value, evaluation window, sample size, data source, exclusions, and owner. Distinguish absolute from percentage change. Do not imply causality when other changes could explain results. Label estimates and simulations clearly.

## Drafting prompt
> Create a case study using only supplied and approved source material. Audience: [role]. Industry/process: [context]. Preserve exact metrics, dates, scope, and caveats. Label pilot, simulation, proposed architecture, and production deployment accurately. Insert [EVIDENCE REQUIRED] for missing facts. Do not invent quotes, ROI, accuracy, savings, uptime, or implementation details.

## Technical review
Confirm architecture and integrations match the actual deployment; model/task and metrics are correctly described; sample size and operating conditions are stated where material; performance definitions and limitations are clear; human review, override, and fallback are explained; security and on-prem/edge/cloud claims are approved; safety-critical control claims have engineering approval.

## Publication checklist
- Customer name, logo, quote, and images approved.
- Product claims approved by product/engineering.
- Legal, privacy, security, and compliance review completed where required.
- Every external claim has a verified citation.
- CTA reflects a real offer.

## Industry.AI format
Context → Operational problem → Asset/process → Data inputs → Solution → Human/AI role → Integration → Measured outcome → KPIs → Limitations → Approved product mapping → Next step.
